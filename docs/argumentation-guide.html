Argumentation Walkthrough

How to disagree, respond, and endorse on Xudanu — without ever touching the numbers you're arguing about.

The one thing to know first: making any kind of link — including a Disagreement — never modifies either document's text. If a passage says $12.40, it still says $12.40 after you dispute it, respond to it, and endorse it. Arguments live in the connections between documents, not inside them. You cannot corrupt a price by arguing about it.

1. What you're looking at

Open a document and click the Links tab (right panel). Three zones:

In the text itself

Disputed passages carry a colored underline (the link type's color). The margin bars mark each line's connection activity: left side = links this document makes, right side = links aimed at it. A small amber chip ×N means N links share that passage — hover any bar for details.

2. Disagree with something (the safe way)

Suppose a budget passage says duralum funculator cost is $12.40 and your market data says otherwise.

  1. Write your criticism as its own document. “Consumer Division Position — the aerospace case should not set the consumer bill of materials.” One claim, in your words. Your document; nobody else's.
  2. Select the exact passage in your document that makes the claim (“the consumer segment is price-sensitive”).
  3. Click Link → target the budget document's disputed passage → type Disagreement.
  4. Done. Their document now shows a ⚑ chain row with your work, awaiting response.
Why your own document? Xudanu's argument model is extrinsic: criticism lives in the critic's document, connected passage-to-passage. The disputed text is never edited — anyone can compare claims side by side, forever, and the history of who-disputed-what is preserved.

3. Respond to a dispute

You're reading the plan and see Consumer Division — awaiting response.

  1. Select the passage in your document that answers the criticism (optional — but it makes the response precise).
  2. Click ↩ respond on the chain row.
  3. A Reference link is created from your selection to the criticism, stamped as the official answer. The chain flips to rebutted. No text changed anywhere.

The status is derived, never stored — it always reflects the current link graph, so “disputed vs. rebutted” can't go stale or be gamed by editing a field.

4. Endorse (say “this connection is right”)

See a link you vouch for? Click the ✓ on its row. That's it. Endorsements are how the community sorts arguments: the Links panel and the argument map rank chains by total weight — Miller's “best argument against” is literally the heaviest branch. Withdrawing a vouch is the same button.

5. See the whole argument (Map tab)

The Map tab renders the argument as a tree: your document's contention at top (numbered 1), objections in red below (1.1), responses in green beneath them (1.1.1), counter-disputes in red again. Heavier endorsement = stronger branch. Select any node and type to grow the argument — + objection at the contention, + response under any node. Each addition creates a real document and a real typed link, so the map is always the true state of the debate, never a diagram someone forgot to update.

6. What if I get it wrong?

ActionUndo path
Wrong link typeThe colored type badge is a dropdown — change it in place
Wrong link entirely✕ on the row deletes it (your documents stay)
Wrong endorsementClick ✓ again to withdraw
A document you createdArchive it — reversible, history kept
Anything in the disputed documentNothing to undo — linking never modified it
For teams treating this as financial record: every mutation is journaled to disk before it's acknowledged (WAL, fsync-before-ack) — a crash loses nothing confirmed. Documents are never hard-deleted, only archived, and full revision history with per-author attribution is always available in the History tab. The chain-of-custody indicator (bottom bar, chain valid) verifies content integrity continuously.

7. The AI reader-critic (optional)

✨ Suggest connections asks the server's language model to read the document, its connected works, and the library — then propose typed connections. Proposals are not links: they arrive as cards you confirm, edit, or dismiss. A confirmed proposal becomes an ordinary link that competes for endorsements like any human-made one. Nothing machine-proposed enters the graph unreviewed.

Choosing a local model: the smallest models (1–2B parameters) struggle to produce usable cross-document proposals — expect empty or same-document suggestions. mistral-nemo and up (≈7B+) start generating effective results, including cross-document Disagreements with cited evidence. Set OLLAMA_BASE_URL and OLLAMA_MODEL when starting the server; larger models may take a couple of minutes on first read (model load) — the button says so.
