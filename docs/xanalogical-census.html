The Xanalogical Systems Census

Every known system in the Udanax lineage — and the independents beside it — mapped, verified, and offered back to the community.

What this is. A census of xanalogical systems, started as a community working list on the transclusion.org Zulip in 2026. This page fills in the gaps, adds the systems the working list missed, and records a live/dead check of every web-facing artifact as of 20 November 2026. A census is a snapshot, not a registry: it shows where xanalogical builders were focusing at one datable moment — and dated observations accumulate here rather than being silently overwritten, so over time the page becomes a record of where the field’s attention went, not just what it built. Every entry carries what was verified and how. Corrections are welcome and will be attributed — correct the table, don’t reconstruct the history.

What the table says. Of the Gold lineage specifically, exactly one system is active with a running multi-user product: Xudanu. Its core stays close to the Gold model — and where it diverges, the divergences are documented openly, subsystem by subsystem, complexity comparison by complexity comparison, in both directions. xu60 is an addressing layer (content-addressed immutable URLs, not a docuverse). Everything else in the Gold line is dead, unreleased, or a spec. The independents (Alph, Seed) are alive and well in adjacent territory — different lineage, overlapping ambition.

Ted Nelson’s own browser demos — what remains

The origin designer’s own line of browser demos is itself part of the census, and part of the story. Ted Nelson, describing where the design settled: “The Xanadu design has essentially resolved to one basic document concept: parallel pages with visible connections.” (hyperland.com)

DemoYearWhat it hadState, verified 2026-11-20
XanaduSpace2003–7Full 3D demo (with Robert Adamson Smith): EDL assembly, parallel pages, visible links and transclusionsWindows-only download; development abandoned (“impossible due to the complexity of the code”)
OpenXanadu2014Browser viewer; transclusions only — xanalinks cut to finish in the time availableLive (200)
xuCamb / perma.pub2016The most complete: EDL + xanalinks + visible transclusion + simulated micropayment, users could supply their own EDLsDead: xanadu.com/demo/ is a meta-refresh shell; perma.pub fails TLS (invalid certificate); surviving artifacts are orphaned xanalink text files on hyperland.com
The pattern: the fuller the demo, the deader the link. What remains reachable is the version that cut links; the version that had everything points into a void. (And transclusion.com itself, for contrast, is a parked domain-for-sale page.) The reading public currently has almost nowhere to watch passages visibly connect. This is the gap every active system in the census is, in one way or another, trying to fill.

Timeline, 1988–2026

Thirty-eight years: two origin systems (1988, 1992), one burst of openness (1999), a scatter of descendants — and, since 2025, the busiest the field has ever been.

Where the Gold model is actually documented

The 1999 release was machine-generated C++, famously unreadable. The readable Smalltalk it came from was never given a prose companion. Between gwern’s critique of the idea and machine-derived specs of Green, the clearest public explanation of how the Gold model actually works that we know of is the documentation set on this site — public, free, and built while implementing it:

  • Udanax Gold Heritage — concept mapping — 35+ Gold concepts (enfilades, canopy, clubs, locks, backfollow, H-tree) each traced to its running descendant. The front door if you speak Gold’s vocabulary.
  • Xudanu at a Glance — the clickable whole-system map, every region opening a deep-dive page. The front door if you speak systems vocabulary.
  • Document Space: Tumblers and Enfilades — the position/region/algebra foundation under every address in the system.
  • Transclusion Engine — canopy trees, backfollow queries, content-reuse detection.
  • Transclusion Architecture — this implementation’s variant, compared honestly against Gold’s enfilade/I-stream model.
  • Gold vs. Xudanu complexity — subsystem-by-subsystem Big-O and footprint, derived by reading both codebases — in both directions, including where the 1992 design holds the advantage.
  • Technical Architecture — 15-section deep dive with Big-O analysis of every data structure.
  • The engineering record (dev/ series) — Nelson’s 17 rules with compliance status, the phase documents (mapping algebra → clubs → transclusion queries → federation → governance), the C++→Rust migration notes, and the test registry. Read in sequence, it doubles as a build order for a xanalogical engine — for anyone building their own.
  • Udanax-Gold vs. OpenXanadu vs. Xudanu — three lineages, feature by feature.

Offered so the next builder doesn’t start from zero. If you know of comparable public documentation of the Gold model elsewhere, this section will name it — tell us.

Corrections — how to reach us

This census improves by correction. Wrong dates, dead links reported alive, missed systems, misassigned lineage — all welcome, all attributed. Pick whichever door suits you:

  • Email (no account needed): david@dgjones.info
  • GitHub: open an issue or pull request at github.com/jonesd/xudanu — PR corrections carry your attribution with them
  • Zulip: say so on transclusion.org, where the list began (members)

Live/dead states get re-checked periodically; every entry carries the date it was last verified, so you can see how fresh each observation is before relying on it.
