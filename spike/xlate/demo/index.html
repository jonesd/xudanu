Cross-language transclusion

A spike: one German span, live-windowed into an English document, rendered through a translation edge that rides PROV. Provider: libretranslate.

1 · The document a reader sees

The network keeps its promises in more than one language now. A reader who reads no German still sees the German text — not as a copy, and not as a translation of the document, but as a rendition of one transcluded span ⚙ machine · de→en. The window is live: if the source work is revised, the span address changes, and the rendition is recomputed under a new key — nothing is silently invalidated, and nothing goes stale.
The chip is the contract. It does not say "translate" — it names the provider, the model, and the grade. Machine renditions are labeled machine-gisting until a human (or a quorum) says otherwise. Click-through goes to the original, never the other way round.

5 · What this suggests

Where this goes next. Read-side only — the original is never edited through MT, so author and archive cannot diverge. Per-host providers keep reader text inside the federation's trust boundary (self-hosted LibreTranslate now, any better local model later, same trait). And the cluster finally gets a translation-shaped job: a quorum endorsing a canonical rendition — "faithful to revision 7, checksum verified" — is a real governance decision, signed and sealed like any other.
